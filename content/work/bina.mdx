<Section title="What it does" intro="Hosted by Mistral AI, sponsored by Stanford Law School, Legora, Hector AI, Jus Mundi and Sciences Po.">
  Since the French law of 30 April 2025, an association can bring a collective action on behalf of people hurt by a data breach. When the CNIL sanctions a company, its decision already establishes the fault, the facts and who was affected. Bina finds those decisions, turns each one into a funding brief an association can send, and matches it with litigation funders.

  The hard part is trust. A funder can't act on a brief that misquotes the ruling, so every quote in it is checked by code against the decision before anyone reads it.

<Section title="How it works">
  <Cards>
    <Card title="Radar">Scrapes the CNIL sanctions list every six hours and triages it with rules: 396 decisions scanned, 36 worth a closer look.</Card>
    <Card title="Reading the decision">Reads the PDF's text layer first, exact and free, and falls back to Mistral OCR only for scans. Long decisions get embeddings and retrieval; the 153-page EU decision was taking about 40 seconds to read whole.</Card>
    <Card title="The brief">Mistral Medium extracts the facts at temperature 0, each with its quote. Damages, opt-in rates and the five scores are computed in code from the legal team's tables.</Card>
    <Card title="Funders and chat">Funders are matched criterion by criterion in code, and an agent answers questions about the decision with checked quotes and a visible trace of what it did.</Card>
  </Cards>
</Section>

<Section title="The quote check">
  The model proposes quotes; code decides whether they stand. Each quote is normalised (apostrophes, dashes, hyphenation, spacing) and must appear on the cited page or a neighbouring one. If it doesn't, the checker tries to repair it from the decision's own words, but only when negations and numbers are identical, so a fix can never flip the meaning. Quotes that still fail are dropped from the pitch and marked as not found in the brief and the chat.

  <ProjectFigure slug="bina" />

  On the Free Mobile decision, 16 of 16 quotes in the brief were verified, four of them after repair; on the 153-page EU decision, 14 of 14.
</Section>

<Section title="Hard calls">
  <div>
    <HardCall number="01" title="Code checks every quote; the model only proposes">
      Anything a funder relies on has to be traceable, so verification is deterministic code, not a second model call. Page and paragraph numbers are located by code as well, never taken from the model.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="02" title="The model never does arithmetic">
      Claim values, opt-in rates, the funder's share and the scores come from the legal team's tables in code. The model extracts facts; the numbers are computed, so the same decision always gives the same brief.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="03" title="No probability of winning">
      A CNIL sanction proves a regulatory breach, not liability to each victim. Bina never states a chance of success: it is forbidden in the prompts and filtered out of the answers in code.
    </HardCall>
  </div>
