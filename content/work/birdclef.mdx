<Section title="The problem">
  Sheharyar Ishfaq took on BirdCLEF+ 2026 alone in May 2026, during his first year of the MSc at Sorbonne. BirdCLEF+ 2026 asks which species are calling in a recording. The training set has 35,549 clips from Xeno-canto and iNaturalist, resampled to 32 kHz, covering 206 species. It is badly imbalanced: the largest class has 499 recordings, 25 species have fewer than 10, and four have a single clip.

  The course rule made it harder: classical models only, so every pattern a network would learn from a spectrogram had to be captured by hand-built features first.

<Section title="What I built">
  <Cards>
    <Card title="Audio features">30 librosa features per frame (13 MFCCs, spectral centroid, bandwidth and rolloff, zero-crossing rate, RMS and 12 chroma), summarised over time by six statistics into 180 numbers per clip.</Card>
    <Card title="A fair benchmark">Five models compared with 5-fold stratified cross-validation, scored on macro ROC-AUC so every species counts the same, rare or common.</Card>
    <Card title="Recording metadata">Latitude, longitude, recording rating and source added as four extra features, which lifted the best model from 0.893 to 0.922.</Card>
    <Card title="Ablation and error analysis">Removing the MFCCs drops AUC from 0.897 to 0.758; every other group moves it by under 0.002. Small classes score lowest, and field soundscapes far below clean clips.</Card>
  </Cards>
</Section>

<Section title="Hard calls">
  <div>
    <HardCall number="01" title="A linear model over the stronger ones">
      I expected the tree ensembles to win. They didn't: the non-linear work from raw audio to features is already done before the classifier sees the data, so the bottleneck is not capturing complex patterns but generalising from a few samples per species. A linear model has lower variance, and it came out on top.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="02" title="A linear SVM, not an RBF kernel">
      RBF kernels scale roughly with the square of the training set, which is impractical for 35,000 clips. A linear SVM kept the comparison runnable and still scored 0.867.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="03" title="Location as a prior">
      Latitude and longitude encode where a species can live, and field recorders sit at fixed, known places, so they are available at inference time too. The recording rating is not available for unlabelled audio, and the report says so.
    </HardCall>
  </div>
</Section>

<Section title="What I'd do next">
  The biggest gap is the jump from clean clips to field soundscapes: 25.5% accuracy on clean validation clips against an 8.8% hit rate on soundscape segments. Next would be training on soundscape-like noise, and class weighting or oversampling for the rarest species.
