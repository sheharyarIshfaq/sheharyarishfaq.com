<Section title="The problem">
  Market research decides where media budgets go, but the classic tools are slow and narrow: pre-testing a video with a live panel could cost $50,000, and a customer journey map is often drawn from 15 or 20 interviews. The new shortcut, asking a chatbot about your audience, is fast, but it sounds just as confident when it is guessing.

  Mavera models whole synthetic populations instead, so a team can test a message, a creative or a journey against the spread of a market rather than a handful of people. As its CEO, Jill Axline, puts it: “Decisions shouldn't move faster than evidence.” Every answer has to come with receipts: the sources behind it, the steps taken and how far to trust it.
</Section>

<Section title="Hard calls">
  <div>
    <HardCall number="01" title="Video jobs that pick up where they stopped">
      A long video is cut into chunks, each a 2×2 grid of frames plus its transcript. Finished chunks are kept, so a retry skips them instead of paying for them twice, and each chunk sees at most the five before it, which keeps the context inside the model's limits.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="02" title="Retries that wait for the rate limit">
      Focus groups make many model calls at once. Instead of retrying blindly, the job reads the provider's rate-limit headers and schedules the retry for the moment the limit resets, and it stops at once when the quota itself has run out.
    </HardCall>
    <HardCall number="03" title="Every answer leaves a trace">
      The agent records each phase it goes through, with its duration and what it decided. That trace is the Timeline tab, part of the receipts Mavera promises: a researcher can see why Mave searched, which personas it used and where the time went before putting an answer in front of a client.
    </HardCall>
